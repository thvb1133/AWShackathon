🧠 The classifier that routes this application

Every request you type or speak is first classified into one of five intents by two independent quantum models. Where they agree, the mesh commits to a few specialists. Where they disagree, confidence drops and the search widens. This is not a demonstration bolted on the side — switch it off and the router gets worse.

Training, live

Five one-vs-rest variational circuits, trained by the parameter-shift rule — the same way gradients are obtained on real hardware, because you cannot backpropagate through a physical device.

🔐 BB84 quantum key distribution

Bennett and Brassard, 1984 — the protocol the Micius satellite flew in 2016. Alice encodes each bit in a randomly chosen basis, Bob measures in his own. Where the bases match, the bits match, and those positions become the key.

The security is physical, not mathematical. An eavesdropper must guess a basis too, and every wrong guess randomises the qubit she forwards. Switch Eve on below and watch the error rate jump to about 25% — she cannot hide, because measurement changes what it measures.

Every qubit in this exchange is genuinely prepared, rotated and measured on the simulator. The key that comes out is used for something real: it encrypts your LLM API key at rest on the JARVIS page.

🤝 Entanglement

A Hadamard and a CNOT make a Bell pair. Measure one qubit and the other is decided instantly, every single time — which is the correlation the whole of quantum key distribution rests on.

What this is, and what it is not

  • It is the genuine algorithms: state-vector simulation with exact unitary gates, the ZZ feature map of Havlíček et al. (2019), quantum kernel estimation by the overlap trick, parameter-shift gradients, BB84 with sifting and an abort threshold, and Grover amplitude amplification.
  • It is not a quantum processor. Nothing in a browser can be. A simulated key exchange also cannot give physical secrecy, because Alice and Bob are the same computer — so the key here is used for obfuscation at rest, and this page says so rather than pretending otherwise.
  • Why bother, then? Because the routing genuinely runs on it, the mathematics is genuinely correct, and a claim you can inspect is worth more than one you cannot.
